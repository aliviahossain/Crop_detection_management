# Integrated Pest and Disease Management (IPDM)

Every advisory this system issues follows the IPDM ladder. Chemicals are the
last rung, not the first.

1. **Prevent** - certified disease-free seed, resistant or tolerant varieties,
   crop rotation, field sanitation, drainage, balanced nutrition, correct plant
   spacing.
2. **Monitor** - scout twice weekly, keep pheromone traps, and watch the weather
   risk forecast. You cannot manage what you do not measure.
3. **Decide with a threshold** - act on an economic or action threshold (trap
   counts, DSV accumulation, a fired Smith Period), never on the calendar.
4. **Use non-chemical control first** - rogueing, dehaulming, sanitation,
   biocontrol agents, physical barriers.
5. **Apply chemicals selectively** - correct product, correct label dose,
   correct timing, rotated chemistry groups, full protective equipment,
   pre-harvest interval observed.
6. **Record and review** - log what was applied and whether it worked. That
   record is what makes the next decision better.

# When to refer instead of self-treat

The system automatically flags a case for expert referral when any of these
hold. The farmer advisory then says "get this confirmed" rather than "spray
this".

- **Low model confidence** - the detector is not sure enough to justify a
  chemical recommendation.
- **The detection model is unavailable** or the crop/symptom is outside the
  three potato classes the model was trained on.
- **Conflicting evidence** - the image says one disease but the weather risk
  model points strongly at another.
- **High weather risk with no visible symptom** - a preventive decision worth an
  officer's judgement.
- **Repeated treatment failure** - two correctly applied sprays from different
  chemistry groups have not held. This suggests resistance and needs laboratory
  confirmation, not a third spray.
- **Suspected virus, nematode, bacterial wilt, or an unfamiliar symptom** - these
  need a laboratory, not a fungicide.
- **Severity above roughly 25% of the field**, or many neighbouring fields
  affected - this is an outbreak needing a coordinated district response.

## Where to refer
1. **Village level:** Krishi Sahayak / Agriculture Assistant.
2. **Block level:** Taluka Agriculture Officer (TAO).
3. **District level:** District Superintending Agriculture Officer (SDAO), and
   the district Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK) for diagnosis and soil/plant testing.
4. **State/national:** ICAR-CPRI regional station for potato-specific diagnosis;
   ICAR-NCIPM for pest surveillance escalation.
5. **Kisan Call Centre: 1800-180-1551** (toll-free, multiple Indian languages).

## How to prepare a sample for the laboratory
- Collect leaves showing **early to mid-stage symptoms**, not fully rotted
  tissue - dead tissue is colonised by secondary organisms and is hard to read.
- Include a section of healthy tissue adjacent to the lesion.
- Wrap in **dry newspaper**, not polythene. Sealed plastic rots the sample.
- Label with village, survey number, crop, variety, sowing date, and what has
  already been applied.
- Deliver within 48 hours.

# Follow-up monitoring

Every advisory schedules a follow-up. The purpose is twofold: it protects the
farmer, and it produces the outcome data the system learns from.

- **Late blight:** re-inspect after 5-7 days.
- **Early blight:** re-inspect after 7-10 days.
- **Pests:** check traps after 7 days.
- **Healthy / preventive:** re-photograph in 7 days, or immediately if the risk
  forecast turns HIGH.

At follow-up, record one of: resolved, improving, unchanged, worsened.
**Unchanged or worsened after a correct application escalates the case to an
extension officer automatically** - because a repeated spray with the same
product is exactly the wasteful, residue-generating response the problem
statement asks us to prevent.
