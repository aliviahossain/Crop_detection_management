# Potato Early Blight

## What it is
Early blight is caused by the fungus *Alternaria solani*. It is slower and less
explosive than late blight but is chronic, favours warmer weather, and hits
older, stressed or nitrogen-starved plants hardest. Yield loss comes from
premature defoliation rather than sudden collapse.

## How to confirm it in the field
- Small dark brown to black spots, typically **2-10 mm**, appearing first on the
  **older, lower leaves**.
- **Concentric rings inside the lesion, giving a target-board or bullseye
  appearance.** This is the key diagnostic separating it from late blight.
- A yellow halo often surrounds the lesion.
- No white downy growth on the leaf underside (that would be late blight).
- Lesions stay distinct rather than merging into large water-soaked areas.
- Tuber infection appears as dark, sunken, leathery lesions with a raised
  border.

## Conditions that drive it
Warm (24-29 C) weather with alternating wet and dry spells, and leaf wetness
from dew, rain or sprinkler irrigation. The system accumulates **TOMCAST
Disease Severity Values (DSV)**: 15 accumulated DSV is the conventional spray
threshold, 8 DSV is the scouting threshold.

## Immediate action
1. Scout the lower canopy across several points in the field to estimate how
   much leaf area is affected.
2. Correct nitrogen deficiency - early blight is strongly worsened by an
   under-fed, senescing crop. A well-nourished crop resists it.
3. Remove and destroy severely affected lower leaves and crop debris.
4. Begin the spray schedule if DSV threshold is met or symptoms are spreading
   upward from the lower canopy.

## Chemical management
Dosages are the commonly published ICAR/CPRI extension rates.
**Confirm against the product label and your local KVK before mixing.**

| Product | Dose | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Mancozeb 75% WP | 2.0-2.5 g/litre (approx. 2 kg/ha) | First-line protectant; repeat at 10-day intervals. |
| Chlorothalonil 75% WP | 2.0 g/litre | Good rotation partner to mancozeb. |
| Azoxystrobin 23% SC | 0.5-1.0 ml/litre | Strobilurin; strictly limit to 2 applications per season. |
| Difenoconazole 25% EC | 0.5 ml/litre | Triazole; rotate with a multi-site protectant. |
| Hexaconazole 5% EC | 2.0 ml/litre | Alternative triazole. |

### Resistance management
Strobilurins (azoxystrobin) and triazoles both carry real resistance risk in
*Alternaria*. Alternate chemistry groups every spray and always keep mancozeb or
chlorothalonil in the programme as the multi-site backbone.

## Cultural and preventive practice (IPDM core)
- **Balanced nutrition, especially adequate nitrogen and potassium** - the single
  most effective non-chemical control for this disease.
- Crop rotation of 2-3 years away from potato, tomato and brinjal (all
  *Alternaria* hosts).
- Deep ploughing to bury infected debris; the fungus survives on residue and in
  soil between seasons.
- Avoid overhead irrigation late in the day; keep leaf wetness periods short.
- Remove volunteer potato and solanaceous weeds.
- Use healthy, certified seed tubers.

## When to escalate instead of self-treating
- Symptoms do not clearly show the concentric-ring pattern - it may be late
  blight, a nutrient disorder, or a different *Alternaria* species.
- Rapid defoliation despite a correct spray programme.
- Widespread field-level severity above roughly 25% leaf area.

## Follow-up
Re-inspect 7-10 days after spraying and record whether lesions have spread to
the middle and upper canopy. Persistent upward spread after two correct sprays
means the programme needs changing - refer to an extension officer.
