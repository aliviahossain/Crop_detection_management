# Healthy Potato Crop

## What the result means
The image shows no disease or pest symptoms the model was trained to detect.
**This is not a clean bill of health for the whole field.** The model saw one
photograph of a few leaves. Early infections, root and tuber problems, and
anything outside the three trained classes will not appear here.

## Do not spray
A healthy diagnosis means **no fungicide or insecticide application is
warranted right now**. Calendar spraying on a symptom-free crop with no weather
risk raises cost, leaves residues, and accelerates fungicide resistance in the
district. Follow the weather risk forecast instead of the calendar.

## Keep it healthy
- **Scout twice a week**, walking a diagonal across the field and inspecting the
  lower canopy. Photograph anything unusual and submit it.
- Watch this system's weather risk forecast. When late blight risk turns MEDIUM
  or HIGH, apply a protectant *before* symptoms appear - that is the entire
  point of forecasting.
- Maintain balanced nutrition; avoid excess nitrogen, which creates a dense
  humid canopy that favours blight.
- Keep ridges well earthed-up to protect developing tubers.
- Irrigate early in the day so the canopy dries before nightfall.
- Maintain field drainage; remove volunteer potato plants and cull piles.
- Keep pheromone traps in place if tuber moth is a local problem, and check them
  weekly.

## When to submit another photograph
- Immediately, if you see any leaf spotting, wilting, or yellowing.
- Whenever the risk forecast reaches HIGH.
- Otherwise, roughly weekly through the tuber initiation and bulking stages,
  which is when the crop has the most to lose.
