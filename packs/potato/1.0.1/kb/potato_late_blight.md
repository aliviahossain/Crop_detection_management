# Potato Late Blight

## What it is
Late blight is caused by the oomycete *Phytophthora infestans*. It is the single
most destructive potato disease in India and can destroy an unprotected crop in
7-10 days once conditions turn favourable. It is the disease the Smith Period
weather rule was written for.

## How to confirm it in the field
- Water-soaked, irregular pale-green to brown lesions starting at leaf tips and
  margins, spreading inward.
- A **white downy fungal growth on the underside of the leaf**, most visible in
  early morning humid conditions. This is the key field diagnostic that
  separates late blight from early blight.
- Lesions have **no concentric rings** (early blight does).
- Dark brown to black lesions on stems and petioles; infected tubers show
  reddish-brown dry rot spreading irregularly from the skin inward.
- A characteristic musty smell in a heavily infected field.

## Conditions that drive it
Cool, wet, humid weather: 10-20 C with relative humidity above 90% for 10+
hours. Two consecutive such days constitute a **Smith Period** and mean
infection has almost certainly taken place.

## Immediate action (first 24-48 hours)
1. **Do not wait for spread.** Late blight doubles roughly every 3-5 days under
   favourable weather.
2. Remove and destroy (bury deep or burn) heavily infected plants and any cull
   or volunteer potato piles near the field - these are the inoculum source.
3. Stop evening/overhead irrigation; irrigate early in the day so the canopy
   dries before nightfall.
4. Begin the spray schedule below.

## Chemical management
Dosages below are the commonly published ICAR/CPRI extension rates.
**Always confirm against the product label and your local Krishi Vigyan Kendra
(KVK) before mixing.** Rates are given per litre of water for a knapsack
sprayer; typical spray volume is 500-1000 L/ha depending on canopy size.

### Protectant (before disease appears, or when risk forecast is HIGH)
| Product | Dose | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Mancozeb 75% WP | 2.0-2.5 g/litre (approx. 2 kg/ha) | Contact/multi-site protectant. Repeat every 7-10 days. Low resistance risk. |
| Chlorothalonil 75% WP | 2.0 g/litre | Alternative multi-site protectant. |
| Copper oxychloride 50% WP | 2.5-3.0 g/litre | Useful where organic-compatible options are preferred. |

### Curative / systemic (only once symptoms are present or a Smith Period has fired)
| Product | Dose | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Cymoxanil 8% + Mancozeb 64% WP | 3.0 g/litre (approx. 1.5-2.0 kg/ha) | Standard first curative spray. |
| Metalaxyl-M 4% + Mancozeb 64% WP | 2.5 g/litre | Do not use more than twice per season - high resistance risk. |
| Dimethomorph 50% WP | 1.0 g/litre, tank-mixed with mancozeb | Rotate with the above. |
| Fenamidone 10% + Mancozeb 50% WG | 3.0 g/litre | Alternative rotation partner. |

### Resistance management (this part is not optional)
- **Alternate FRAC groups between sprays.** Never apply the same systemic
  fungicide twice in a row. *P. infestans* develops metalaxyl resistance very
  quickly and once it does, that chemistry is lost for the whole district.
- Cap systemic sprays at 2-3 per season; fill the gaps with protectants.
- Always tank-mix a systemic with a multi-site protectant.

## Cultural and preventive practice (IPDM core)
- Plant **certified disease-free seed tubers**; this is the highest-return
  single intervention.
- Prefer moderately resistant varieties where available: Kufri Girdhari, Kufri
  Himalini, Kufri Badshah. Kufri Jyoti, Kufri Lauvkar and Kufri Chandramukhi
  are more susceptible.
- Adequate earthing-up (ridge height) to stop sporangia washing down onto
  tubers.
- Avoid excess nitrogen, which produces a dense humid canopy.
- Maintain field drainage; waterlogged patches become blight foci.
- **Dehaulm (cut and remove haulms) 7-10 days before harvest** if blight is
  present, and let tuber skins set before lifting. This is the main way to keep
  the disease out of storage.
- Harvest in dry conditions; do not store visibly infected tubers.

## When to escalate instead of self-treating
- The model confidence is low, or the symptoms do not clearly match either
  blight description.
- The infection is already above roughly 10% of the field.
- Sprays applied correctly are not holding - suspect fungicide resistance, which
  needs a lab and a changed programme, not a repeat spray.
- Send a sample to the nearest KVK or the state plant-health clinic.

## Follow-up
Re-inspect 5-7 days after the first spray. Record whether new lesions have
appeared. If lesion count is still rising after two correctly applied sprays
from different FRAC groups, escalate.
