# Potato Tuber Moth (*Phthorimaea operculella*)

## Why it matters
The most damaging insect pest of potato in Maharashtra, in the field and
especially in storage. Larvae mine leaves and petioles, then bore into exposed
tubers. Losses in poorly managed country stores can be very high.

## Monitoring (do this before considering any spray)
- Install **pheromone traps at 20-25 per hectare**, and check them weekly.
- The degree-day model in this system predicts emergence peaks: approximately
  **360 degree-days above a 10 C base** completes one generation. Time your trap
  checks and any intervention to that peak.
- **Action threshold: a sustained average above roughly 20 moths per trap per
  week.** Below that, cultural control alone is appropriate.

## Management, in order of preference
1. **Deep earthing-up.** Exposed tubers are how the pest gets in. Keeping ridges
   high and soil cracks closed prevents most field damage. This is the single
   most effective control.
2. Irrigate to keep soil from cracking during tuber bulking.
3. Harvest promptly once the crop matures; do not leave lifted tubers in the
   field overnight.
4. Remove and destroy crop residue and volunteer plants after harvest.
5. **Storage protection** - where most damage happens:
   - Store only clean, undamaged, dry tubers.
   - Cover the tuber heap with a **2-3 cm layer of dry chopped *Lantana camara*
     or neem leaves**, a long-standing CPRI recommendation.
   - Keep stores cool, dark and ventilated. Cold storage at 2-4 C stops the pest
     entirely.
   - Use pheromone traps inside the store to monitor.
6. **Biological control:** *Bacillus thuringiensis* var. *kurstaki* formulations
   applied to tubers before storage; granulosis virus (PoGV) where available.
7. **Chemical control, only if trap counts exceed the threshold** and confirmed
   with an extension officer. Follow the CIB&RC-registered label for potato and
   observe the pre-harvest interval. Do not apply insecticide to tubers intended
   for storage or seed unless the label specifically permits it.

# Aphid Complex (virus vectors)

## Why it matters
Aphids (*Myzus persicae* and others) do limited direct feeding damage but
transmit potato virus Y (PVY) and potato leaf roll virus (PLRV). In **seed
potato** production they are the primary concern, because a virus-infected seed
crop degenerates and carries the loss into every field that plants it.

## Monitoring
- Count aphids on 100 compound leaves sampled across the field, weekly from
  tuber initiation.
- **Seed crop threshold: 20 aphids per 100 compound leaves.** At this point the
  seed crop should be dehaulmed to stop further virus spread, regardless of how
  the foliage looks.
- Degree-day accumulation above a 4.4 C base indicates flight build-up.

## Management
- Plant certified virus-free seed.
- Rogue out (remove) plants showing mosaic, mottling, or leaf roll as soon as
  they are seen.
- **Time the crop so bulking finishes before the aphid population peaks** - the
  standard seed-plot technique.
- **Dehaulm at the aphid threshold** for seed crops. This is the core of the
  seed plot technique and matters more than any spray.
- Conserve natural enemies - ladybird beetles, syrphid larvae and parasitoids
  suppress aphids well when broad-spectrum insecticides are not used.
- Chemical control is rarely justified for a ware (table) crop; for seed crops,
  use only a CIB&RC-registered product at label dose after consulting an
  extension officer.

## When to escalate
- Trap counts or aphid counts far above threshold across many fields - this is a
  district-level outbreak and needs a coordinated CROPSAP-style response, not
  individual spraying.
- Suspected virus symptoms in a seed crop - requires laboratory confirmation.
