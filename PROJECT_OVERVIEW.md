# CropGuard Maharashtra — Project Overview

**Smart India Hackathon 2026 · Problem Statement PS26131 — Crop Disease & Pest Detection System**
Government of Maharashtra · Maharashtra State Innovation Society (Dept. of Skills, Employment, Entrepreneurship and Innovation)
**Category:** Software · **Theme:** Agriculture, FoodTech & Rural Development

> A proactive, offline-capable crop-health platform for farmers and agricultural officers, currently focused on **potato**. It pairs on-device camera scanning with weather-driven disease forecasting, safety-gated multilingual advisories, and a geospatial dashboard for officials.

---

## 1. Problem Statement

### 1.1 The problem, in the field

- Farmers often detect crop diseases only after visible damage has occurred.
- Extension workers manage large areas, making immediate expert advice scarce.
- Crucial risk factors like weather, crop stage, and soil conditions are not translated into actionable farm-level alerts.
- This delay leads to excessive pesticide use, increased costs, and significant crop yield losses.

### 1.2 Official problem statement (PS26131)

> Farmers often recognise crop diseases or pest infestations only after visible damage has spread. Extension staff may cover large areas, while laboratory diagnosis and expert advice may not be immediately available. Weather, crop stage, variety, soil condition and local pest history influence risk, but these inputs are rarely combined into actionable farm-level alerts. Incorrect diagnosis may lead to delayed treatment, excessive or inappropriate pesticide use, increased cultivation cost, residue concerns and yield loss. The challenge is to provide timely, reliable and locally relevant detection, forecasting and management support.

**Expected solution (official).** A farmer- and extension-worker-friendly crop-health system supporting image-based symptom identification, pest-trap or sensor inputs, weather-based risk forecasting, geospatial hotspot mapping, expert validation, and multilingual advisories. It should recommend integrated pest and disease management (IPDM) actions, safe input usage, referral to extension officers or laboratories, and follow-up monitoring; learn from field confirmations; and provide dashboards for agriculture officials.

**Expected outcomes.** Earlier detection, reduced crop loss, more targeted pesticide use, faster extension response, improved surveillance coverage, and better planning of preventive interventions.

---

## 2. Solution

CropGuard Maharashtra is a proactive crop-health platform for farmers and agricultural officers, currently focused on potatoes. It delivers the full loop the problem statement asks for — detect, forecast, advise, verify, and follow up — with every required capability implemented and traceable to code (see [`docs/PS_TRACEABILITY.md`](PS_TRACEABILITY.md)).

**For farmers**

- **On-device camera scanning** for instant disease detection — runs live, in-browser, with no per-frame server call.
- **Photo upload** as an alternative to live scanning, routed through the same safety pipeline.
- **Weather-driven forecasting** that predicts disease risk *before* symptoms appear.
- **Localized IPDM advisories** in Marathi, Hindi, Bengali, and English, including safe-use guidance and an explicit "do not spray" when the crop is healthy.
- **Follow-up monitoring** that tracks whether a recommended treatment actually worked, and escalates repeated failures to a laboratory instead of prescribing a third spray.

**For extension workers and officials**

- **Geospatial hotspot mapping** — a grid view an officer can read cell-by-cell plus a true density heatmap that localizes to village clusters.
- **Pest-trap / sensor ingestion** as a second surveillance signal that feeds tuber-moth risk and appears on the map.
- **Expert validation queue** where an officer confirms or corrects each AI diagnosis; confirmed cases become training data.
- **Officer dashboard** with district-level trends, high-risk district ranking, and a live-only / demo data switch.
- **Referral and triage logic** that hands low-confidence or conflicting cases to a human rather than naming a pesticide.

**System-wide principles**

- **Safety first:** chemical doses are parsed from human-reviewed knowledge-base tables, never generated by an AI.
- **Honest by default:** the system reports its own degraded components and separates real faults from deliberate design limits.
- **Offline-ready:** live scanning and multilingual advisories work with no network and no paid APIs.

## 3. Tech Stack

Technologies grouped by domain.

### Frontend
- **React** (with **Vite**) — farmer flow, officer dashboard, review queue
- **Leaflet** / **Mapbox** — geospatial hotspot mapping and density heatmaps
- **onnxruntime-web** (WebAssembly) — offline, in-browser camera scanning
- **Recharts** — dashboard charts and trends

### Backend
- **FastAPI** — REST API and inference serving layer
- **SQLite** / **PostgreSQL** — cases, follow-ups, sensor readings, training samples, weather cache
- **Uvicorn** — ASGI server

### AI & ML — Vision
- **YOLOv8s** — visual disease detection, exported to **ONNX** for CPU inference
- **ONNX Runtime** — CPU serving on the backend; **onnxruntime-web** for the browser mirror
- **Ultralytics + PyTorch** — training only (on cloud GPU, never on the dev laptop)

### AI & ML — Risk Forecasting
- **Rule-based agronomic models** — Smith Period, Beaumont Period, TOMCAST, and degree-day accumulation
- **XGBoost + SHAP** — secondary risk layer (inactive by design until real outbreak data exists)
- **OpenWeatherMap** — free-tier weather feed (current conditions + 5-day forecast)

### Advisory Engine
- **LangGraph** — Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) pipeline: `retrieve → compose → safety → localize`
- **ChromaDB** — vector retrieval, with a pure-Python **BM25** fallback when Chroma is unavailable
- **Human-reviewed markdown knowledge base** — the authoritative source of every dose and safe-use rule

### Localization
- **Message catalog** — 49 messages × 4 languages (English, Marathi, Hindi, Bengali), zero-cost, no API key

## 4. Target Audience and Societal Impact

**Audience.** Designed for farmers, local extension workers, and state agriculture officials.

**Impact.**

- **Reduces crop loss** by predicting threats early — a fired Smith Period is an infection event, not a visible one, so the warning arrives before symptoms.
- **Promotes targeted pesticide use** by specifically advising farmers when *not* to spray healthy crops, withholding dose tables on low confidence, and routing repeated treatment failures to a laboratory instead of another spray.
- **Empowers officials** to deploy staff efficiently based on verified hotspot data, with confirmed cases weighted above unverified model output.
- **Improves surveillance coverage** — every photo becomes a georeferenced case, and pest-trap ingestion adds a second signal stream.

## 5. How It Works

1. Farmers use the **live scanner** or **upload a photo**, or the system runs a **proactive weather check** with no image at all.
2. The backend assesses the image and weather data, routing every case — photo-triggered or proactive — through a single **safety triage** gate in `pipeline.py`, so a weather alert cannot bypass a rule a photo case obeys.
3. The system **retrieves guidance from a human-reviewed knowledge base** to generate a localized advisory, returning the exact KB sections it drew from as citations.
4. The **live camera filters out blurry frames** (variance-of-Laplacian blur and exposure scoring) and requires a **consensus of multiple clear frames** — default 6 of 10 good frames at ≥55% mean confidence — before issuing a verdict, preventing false alarms from a single lucky frame.
5. Nothing is stored until the farmer presses **Accept**, which sends that exact frame through the full `/detect` pipeline — same advisory, same triage, same follow-up as a photo upload. Discarded scans leave no record.
6. An **officer reviews** the case, confirming or correcting the diagnosis; a confirmed case becomes a training sample, and a failed follow-up auto-escalates the case.

## 6. Unique Selling Proposition (USP)

- **Safety First.** Extracts chemical guidelines strictly from human-reviewed markdown tables instead of letting AI hallucinate pesticide doses. The safety block is attached in a separate pipeline node *after* composition, so no path can accidentally omit it.
- **Hyperlocal microclimate sensing (experimental).** Turns the phone's live camera into a passive, in-field **airflow** proxy: classical pixel math cancels camera shake and reads the independent leaf motion that survives, grading canopy airflow as *still / light / breezy* — a signal a distant weather station cannot see. Still air means dew lingers, so **leaf wetness** stretches, which is exactly the input the Smith Period (≥11 h) and TOMCAST blight models read; the reading feeds risk where it actually matters, on today and forecast days that already had dew. Paired with soil-drainage and waterlogging modelling (a waterlogged canopy stays wet longer → higher blight pressure), it localises forecasting to the plant in front of the farmer. An honesty guardrail keeps it safe: a breeze may *raise* concern but can never "un-fire" a blight rule that already met the observed humidity, and every airflow adjustment is recorded as experimental and auditable.
- **Offline Ready.** Runs live camera scanning and multilingual advisories (Marathi, Hindi, Bengali) offline without expensive API calls. The WASM runtime is served from the app's own origin — not a CDN — precisely so the offline claim is real.
- **Honest AI.** Explicitly separates broken components (`degraded`) from deliberate design limitations (`by_design`) on the dashboard, so a judge, officer, or teammate never has to guess whether a number came from a real model or a fallback. Nothing fabricates a result to look complete.
- **A learning flywheel that improves in the field.** Every case an officer confirms or corrects in the review queue becomes a labelled `TrainingSample`, so the system's own field verdicts turn into training data for the next model — directly satisfying the PS's "learns from field confirmations". `ml/export_feedback.py` packages these for a retraining run and warns when a batch is too small or skewed to one district, and the XGBoost risk layer stays dormant until enough confirmed cases exist to train it honestly. The retrain is a **deliberate, human-in-the-loop step, not blind auto-training** — because retraining on unexamined field data is how a model quietly degrades. The AI gets better as it is used, without ever being allowed to teach itself something wrong.
- **Two decoders, one behaviour.** The browser (JavaScript) and server (Python) YOLO decoders are tested against each other case-for-case, including one test that runs a real ONNX model through onnxruntime-web to confirm both produce the identical box.
- **Asymmetric cost, encoded.** Disease classes are tuned for recall (a missed blight costs the field) and `healthy` for precision (a false "healthy" is how delayed treatment happens) — per class, not one global threshold.

## 8. Datasets

- The model is trained on three potato classes: **early blight** (*Alternaria solani*), **late blight** (*Phytophthora infestans*), and **healthy**.
- Combines high-volume laboratory photos (**PlantVillage**, ~1,200 lab images after capping and balancing) with cluttered, real-world field images (**PlantDoc / cropguard-field-potato**, 343 field images) — roughly **1,500+ images combined** across the two training runs.
- Uses strict, deterministic **stratified splitting** (exact per-class quotas by content hash, no train→val leakage) and **oversampling** to correct a severe imbalance against the critical "healthy" class — measured **6.56:1 → 1.00:1**, printed before and after.

> **The caveat that matters most.** PlantVillage is *laboratory* imagery — one leaf on a uniform grey background. A model trained on it alone scores ~0.995 on its own split and degrades on a real phone photo. The pipeline warns loudly when a validation split contains zero field images, and `evaluate.py` reports lab and field mAP **separately** and refuses to present a lab number as field accuracy. Merging more field-condition images is the single highest-value outstanding task.

## 9. Model Results

Two runs: a PlantVillage-only base, then a fine-tune that mixes in field images at a 10× lower learning rate to avoid catastrophic forgetting.

**Fine-tuned model — combined validation (lab + field, 254 images):**

| Class | Precision | Recall | mAP50 | mAP50-95 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| all | 0.917 | 0.869 | 0.928 | 0.795 |
| potato_early_blight | 0.947 | 0.911 | 0.971 | 0.880 |
| potato_late_blight | 0.906 | 0.904 | 0.942 | 0.800 |
| potato_healthy | 0.897 | 0.793 | 0.871 | 0.705 |

Lab-only validation is retained at **mAP50 0.995** — confirming the fine-tune did not forget the PlantVillage signal. The 34 field images in the val split are too few for a reliable standalone field metric; expanding the field dataset is the next increment.

**Active per-class thresholds** (tuned against the asymmetric cost, loaded by the backend at startup):

| Class | Threshold | Objective |
|---|---|---|
| potato_early_blight | 0.294 | F2 (recall-weighted) |
| potato_late_blight | 0.478 | F2 (recall-weighted) |
| potato_healthy | 0.580 | F0.5 (precision-weighted) |

## 10. Real-Life Deployment, Cost, and Results

- **Cost Efficiency.** Runs ONNX models on standard **CPUs** instead of expensive GPUs, relies on **free-tier** weather APIs, and uses **zero-cost local translation catalogs**. Live scanning runs in the browser, so there is no per-frame server cost at all.
- **Results.** Disease detection is tuned for **high recall**, while healthy-crop verification is tuned for **high precision** — the two failure modes are priced differently on purpose.
- **Continuous Improvement.** The system safely guides actions and improves as expert-reviewed cases feed back into the training loop (`ml/export_feedback.py`), which is a deliberate manual step — retraining on unexamined field data is how a model quietly degrades.

## 11. Future Scope & Scalability

**Crop and disease coverage**

- **More crops and classes.** The architecture generalizes; adding a crop is a dataset-and-retrain task — extend `ml/data.yaml`, `taxonomy.py`, and the knowledge base — with no architectural change. Tomato is the natural next crop (it shares late blight and the Smith Period).
- **Dedicated field metric.** Expand the field-image dataset to produce a standalone field-condition validation split, so accuracy is quoted on real phone photos rather than lab imagery.

**Intelligence and forecasting**

- **Activate the XGBoost + SHAP risk layer** once enough real Indian outbreak data accrues (from CROPSAP Maharashtra bulletins, ICAR/NCIPM records, or the system's own confirmed cases). `scripts/export_risk_dataset.py` already builds a leakage-safe training set from confirmed cases; the layer is capped at ±0.20 so it can refine but never override a validated agronomic rule.
- **Real weather history.** Backfill the Smith Period window from a paid or archival weather source instead of the system's own accumulating cache.

**Platform and reach**

- **Native / PWA packaging** for true offline install on low-end Android phones, with model updates over the air.
- **Role-based authentication** before officer endpoints and the review queue are exposed in a real deployment.
- **Hardware trap integration.** The sensor ingestion endpoint already accepts a simple schema; wire it to real IoT pest traps in place of the current mocked/simulated feed.
- **Horizontal scaling.** Migrate SQLite → PostgreSQL, move inference behind a worker pool, and shard the case table by district for state-wide rollout.
- **Automated retraining pipeline** with guardrails (batch-size and district-skew warnings already exist) once field-confirmation volume justifies it.

## 12. Limitations of the Prototype

Stating these is part of the design, not an omission.

- The current system is strictly limited to **potato crops**, three classes.
- The advanced **XGBoost machine-learning risk layer remains inactive** until enough real-world Indian outbreak data is collected to train it honestly. The training script refuses to produce a model from insufficient data (< 200 rows) or below 0.6 AUC. The agronomic models carry the forecasting on their own meanwhile.
- **PlantVillage is lab imagery** — every metric from a PlantVillage-only run is a *lab* metric, reported as such and never presented as field accuracy.
- All chemical doses in the knowledge base carry `review_status: needs_local_validation` and **require formal validation by local agricultural extension offices** (against the CIB&RC product label and the local KVK) before real-world deployment.
- **No authentication** in this build — officer endpoints are open; a real deployment needs role-based access first.
- **Weather history is cached, not backfilled** — free-tier OpenWeatherMap has no history API, so the Smith Period looks back over the system's own accumulating cache and reports how much of the window is synthetic.
