# Safe Input Usage

This guidance attaches to **every** advisory that recommends a chemical input.
The problem statement calls out excessive and inappropriate pesticide use,
residue concerns and cultivation cost as harms to be reduced - these rules are
how the recommendation stays safe.

## Before you buy
- Buy only from a **licensed dealer** and insist on a **printed bill**. Without
  a bill there is no recourse if the product is spurious.
- Check the product is **CIB&RC-registered for use on potato for this specific
  disease or pest**. A product registered on another crop is not approved here.
- Check the expiry date and that the seal is intact.
- **Never buy or use a banned or restricted pesticide.** If a shop offers a
  product because it is "stronger", refuse and report it to the agriculture
  department.

## Before you spray
- Read the label. The label dose is the legal dose. **A higher dose does not
  work better** - it raises residue, cost, and resistance without improving
  control.
- Wear protection: full-sleeve clothing, rubber gloves, boots, a face mask or
  respirator, and eye protection. Most Indian pesticide poisonings come from
  spraying without this.
- Do not let children, pregnant women, or anyone unwell handle or mix pesticide.
- Calibrate the sprayer. Know how many litres your knapsack actually delivers
  per acre before mixing.
- Mix in the open air, never inside the house or near a well, canal, or animal
  drinking point.
- Use a stick to stir, never bare hands.

## While spraying
- Spray **with the wind at your back**, never into the wind.
- Spray in the **early morning or late evening**, not in the midday heat and not
  when rain is expected within 4-6 hours.
- Do not eat, drink, smoke or chew during spraying.
- Never blow into a blocked nozzle with your mouth - clean it with water and a
  soft brush.
- Do not spray when farm workers, children or grazing animals are in or beside
  the field.

## After spraying
- Wash hands, face and all exposed skin with soap before eating. Wash spray
  clothing separately from household clothes.
- **Observe the re-entry interval** printed on the label - typically 24 hours -
  before anyone works in the field again.
- **Observe the pre-harvest interval (PHI)** before harvesting. This is the
  single most important rule for residue safety and for export/market
  acceptance. Mancozeb on potato is commonly 7-15 days depending on the label;
  always use the figure on your specific product.
- **Triple-rinse empty containers**, add the rinse water to the spray tank,
  puncture the container so it cannot be reused, and dispose of it as the label
  directs. Never reuse a pesticide container for water, food, or fuel.
- Store leftover product in its original labelled container, locked, away from
  food, feed and children.

## Record keeping
Write down for every application: date, product name, batch number, dose, area
sprayed, and the person who sprayed. This record is what lets an extension
officer diagnose a failure, proves compliance for market access, and is required
if a residue question ever arises.

## In case of exposure or poisoning
- Move the person to fresh air, remove contaminated clothing, wash the skin with
  soap and water.
- **Take the pesticide container or its label to the hospital** - it tells the
  doctor the antidote.
- Call the nearest primary health centre immediately. Do not induce vomiting
  unless the label or a doctor says to.
- National Poisons Information Centre (AIIMS, New Delhi): **1800-116-117**.

## Reducing chemical use overall
Chemical control is the last step of IPDM, not the first. Resistant varieties,
certified seed, rotation, drainage, balanced nutrition, sanitation, traps and
biocontrol come first, and a weather-driven forecast tells you when a spray is
actually justified. Every spray you can skip safely is money saved and residue
avoided.
